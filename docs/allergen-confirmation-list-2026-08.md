# Allergen list for the kitchen to confirm — August 2026

*Companion to Q3 of the owner questions.*

We worked out the allergen information for your ingredients from the technical sheets you
sent and from standard food references. That's an honest starting point, but it's
second-hand — it's the platform's reading, not your kitchen's word.

**56 entries across 39 ingredients need your kitchen to confirm, correct, or remove them.**
Everything else in your list is either obvious enough to stand on its own or has nothing to
declare.

Right now every item below shows customers "not yet confirmed by the kitchen". That's
truthful, but it's weaker than it needs to be, and a customer with a serious allergy reads
it as a reason to order elsewhere.

**Two words used throughout:**

- **Definitely in it** — the ingredient contains this, always. A customer allergic to it
  cannot eat the dish.
- **Might be in it** — depends on the brand or the batch. A customer allergic to it has to
  decide for themselves.

Turning a "might" into either a "definitely" or a "nothing" is the single most useful thing
you can do here, and most of them come down to reading a label.

## Two things worth a second look

Neither is urgent. Both affect what your customers are told.

### We may be declaring more than we need to

Three items are flagged as **tree nuts** that aren't on the European list you're working to:
**coconut** (and coconut milk, water, sugar), **pine nuts**, and **chestnuts**. The American
rules do count coconut as a tree nut; the European rules name eight specific nuts and none
of these three is among them.

Keeping the flags is the cautious choice and nobody will fault you for it. The cost is that
a nut-allergic customer is steered away from coconut and pesto dishes they could probably
eat. **Keep the caution, or follow the European list?** Your call — say the word either way.

### We may be declaring less than we need to

A handful of ingredients are recorded as having **no allergens at all**, where that's worth
checking. An ingredient wrongly marked clean is more dangerous than one marked with an
unconfirmed "might".

| Ingredient | Worth checking because |
|---|---|
| Chocolate | Almost all chocolate has milk and soy lecithin, and much of it is made on lines shared with nuts |
| Oats | Oats are routinely milled alongside wheat; European rules treat them as a gluten cereal unless certified otherwise |
| Turkey bacon | Processed meats often use milk or soy protein as binders |
| Gluten free flour | The claim is only as good as the supplier's certificate — do you have it on file? |

Ketchup, sriracha and the jams are usually clean, but check for sulphites if the brand is
one you're not sure of.
