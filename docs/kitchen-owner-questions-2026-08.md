Everything that could be built without you has been built. What's left are business
decisions only you can make: how you take money, how food gets out the door, what you're

## First: two things holding up finished work

### Q1. Who at the kitchen owns the account?

We need one named person at your kitchen — name and an email address they check — to be
the account holder. Until someone holds that seat, nobody at the kitchen can see cost
figures, correct a recipe, confirm allergen information, or add staff.

Two pieces of finished work are sitting behind this: a sauce recipe correction that's
identified and ready, and your kitchen's own sign-off on two ingredients' allergen
information. Both go through the moment there's a person to do them.

**We need:** a name and email. They can add the rest of your staff themselves afterwards.

### Q2. The items with no price

Some rows on your product list can't be sold because there's no price to sell them at:

- a handful that came in blank,
- a few marked "depends on the day",
- and a couple sold in both 0.5 kg and 1 kg packs, where only one price was given.

For the market-priced ones, pick how you want them handled:

- **Fixed price you review monthly** — simplest, customers see a price and buy.
- **Price on request** — customer asks, you quote, nothing shows publicly.
- **Leave them off sale** — they stay internal only.

**We need:** a price per item, or "price on request", or "leave it off". And for the
dual-pack items, a price for each size.

### Q3. Your kitchen's sign-off on allergens

We worked out the allergen information for your ingredients from standard food references.
That's honest but it's second-hand — it isn't your kitchen saying so.

**The list is attached** — 56 entries across 39 ingredients, sorted into three jobs: five
minutes of confirming the obvious, a label-reading pass over the 17 products you buy in, and
ten of your own preparations that have no written recipe. See
*Allergen list for the kitchen to confirm*.

The ones that matter most are anything you buy in ready-made rather than make yourself:
sauces, mustards, vinegars, pickles, dried fruit, stocks. Their recipe is theirs, and only
their label tells you what's really in it.

**We need:** the name of the person at the kitchen who owns this (head chef? quality
person?), and a realistic date they can work through the list. Until then, every item on it
carries "not yet confirmed by the kitchen" rather than "confirmed".

**Already settled, no action:** you serve Lebanon now with possible Middle East expansion,
and the European allergen list stays the standard we hold you to.

## Money

### Q4. How do you want to be paid?

Right now the only way a customer can pay is cash on delivery. Nothing else is connected.

**a) Which methods do you want?** Tick all that apply:

- Cash on delivery (already running)
- Cards online
- Local wallet / money-transfer services
- Bank transfer (mostly for business customers)

**b) If cards:** which processor? The usual routes are the one your bank offers, or an
independent card processor. If you already have a merchant account for the physical
kitchen, tell us who it's with — reusing it is cheaper and faster than opening a new one.

**c) Where does the customer type their card number?** Two ways:

- **On the payment company's page** — customer briefly leaves your site to a page that
  looks slightly different. Far less security certification work for you. Recommended.
- **On your own checkout** — smoother, looks like your brand throughout. Costs you an
  annual security audit and ongoing paperwork, because card numbers pass through your
  system.

**d) Are you selling into the UAE, or only Lebanon for now?** It changes which processors
are even an option.

**Nothing about online payment can start until this is answered.** This is the single
biggest thing on the list.

### Q5. Cash on delivery — who counts the money?

Cash is what you're actually running on, and there's no reconciliation behind it today.
An order gets marked "delivered" and nothing tracks whether the cash arrived.

- Who marks an order as **paid** — the driver on the doorstep, the office when the driver
  returns, or you?
- At what moment is it "paid" — cash in hand, or cash counted back at the kitchen?
- What does end of day look like? Does someone count each driver's cash against their
  delivered orders and sign it off?
- What happens when a customer refuses the order at the door, or is short of cash?
- Do drivers carry change, and is there a float?

**We need:** roughly how it works today, even if it's informal. We'll build what you
already do rather than invent a process.

### Q6. Refunds

The system already stops anyone refunding more than was paid. Everything else is your call.

- How long after delivery can a customer ask for money back? (7 days? 24 hours for fresh
  food?)
- Can you refund part of an order — one bad item out of five?
- Who's allowed to approve one: any staff member, a supervisor, only you? Is there an
  amount above which it has to come to you?
- Money back, or credit against their next order? Customer's choice?
- Different rules for a subscription someone wants to quit halfway?

### Q7. Subscriptions — what happens when a plan runs out?

Today a plan simply ends on its last day and nothing happens. The customer has to come back
and buy again.

- Should plans **renew automatically** until the customer cancels, or should each period be
  a fresh purchase?
- If they renew automatically: how much notice before the charge, and how easy should
  cancelling be?
- If you raise prices, does a customer who has been with you a year keep their old price,
  or move to the new one? (Keeping it is good for loyalty and a headache for your margins —
  your call.)

### Q8. Invoices and tax

- Are you VAT-registered? What rate applies to what you sell?
- Does your accountant need invoice numbers in a particular format or sequence? If so, give
  us the format and the last number used, so we don't clash with your books.
- Business customers — corporates, gyms, clinics — do they pay upfront, or on terms? 15
  days? 30?
- **Which currency do you actually settle in — USD, LBP, or both?** If both: when a
  customer pays in one and you price in the other, whose exchange rate applies and on which
  day? This one causes more disputes than anything else on this page, so be specific.

Business invoicing can't go live until this is answered.

### Q9. Store credit and loyalty points — do you want them at all?

Should a customer be able to hold a balance with you, or earn points toward a free meal?

Yes / No / Later. Nothing exists today, and nothing will be built unless you say yes.

## Getting food out the door

### Q10. What are the real stages of an order in your kitchen?

There are kitchen screens built, but they're guessing at your process. Walk us through a
normal day from an order landing to the box leaving.

- What are the actual stages you'd want to see on a screen? (Something like: received →
  prepped → cooked → packed → out for delivery — but tell us yours.)
- Who moves an order from one stage to the next? Does anyone need to sign off?
- **How much can you actually produce?** Meals per day, or per delivery slot?
- When orders go past that number, what should happen — stop taking orders for the slot,
  offer the next day, or take them anyway and you'll cope?
- Are there cut-off times? "Order by 6pm for tomorrow"?

Best answered with your head chef in the room.

### Q11. Delivery

- Your own drivers, a courier company, or both?
- If both: what decides which one takes an order — the area, the size, the day?
- How does a delivery get assigned to a driver: by zone, drivers pick up jobs themselves,
  or someone in the office assigns them?
- Customer isn't home — what's the rule? Try again same day, next day, back to the kitchen,
  leave it with a neighbour? Who eats the cost?
- Do you charge for delivery, and does it change by area or order size?
- Any areas you don't deliver to?

### Q12. What should the customer hear from you, and how?

Which moments are worth a message?

- Order received
- Order confirmed / payment taken
- Out for delivery
- Delivered
- Delayed or something's wrong
- Subscription about to renew or expire

And by which channel — email, SMS, WhatsApp? Email works today. SMS and WhatsApp depend on
Q15 below and cost money per message, so pick the moments that genuinely matter.

## Nutrition

### Q13. Where should the calorie and nutrition numbers come from?

This is the one decision holding back the whole nutrition side of the platform — the meal
planner, the nutrition screens, the dietitian tools. They're all sitting there as
placeholders because we will not invent numbers and put your name on them.

Today the platform shows nothing rather than guess. Your options:

- **A public food database** (free, official, well-respected). Gives you the average
  nutrition of "chicken breast" — not yours specifically. Fine for most kitchens. Thin on
  Middle Eastern and local branded items.
- **A paid commercial database** (a few hundred to a few thousand a year). Better coverage
  of regional and branded products.
- **Lab analysis of your actual dishes** (roughly a few hundred dollars per dish). The only
  option that's genuinely *your* food. Expensive, and it has to be redone when a recipe
  changes.

Many kitchens mix these: lab-test the flagship plans, use a database for everything else.

**Whichever you choose, someone has to do the ongoing work:** matching each of your
ingredients to an entry in that source, and updating it when you change supplier. Is that a
kitchen job, or something you'd rather pay someone to maintain?

**Worth knowing:** the moment you print a calorie count next to a plan, it's a claim you're
answerable for. That's why this one is a decision rather than something we could just do.

## Services you'd need to pay for

### Q15. Text messages and WhatsApp

Do you want customers signing up or confirming with a phone number, and getting delivery
updates by SMS or WhatsApp?

Today it's email only — phone confirmation is switched off, because sending a real text
needs an account with a messaging company and costs per message.

- Do you want it? (It noticeably cuts abandoned signups and "where's my order" calls.)
- Roughly how many messages a month would you expect?
- Do you already have a business WhatsApp number?

### Q16. Sending email

You set up an email service already. Two things to confirm:

- Is it the one to use for real customers, or was it just for testing?
- **What address should customers see mail coming from,** and where should replies go?
  (`orders@yourdomain` and `hello@yourdomain`, or similar.) Whoever controls your domain
  will need to make a small change to prove the address is really yours — otherwise your
  emails land in spam.

### Q17. Signing business contracts

When a business customer signs an agreement with you today, they click to accept and enter
a code sent to them. That's recorded with a timestamp and it holds up as evidence of
agreement — but it is not a formal e-signature, and we label it honestly as such.

Is that enough for your corporate contracts, or do you want a proper e-signature service
(the kind that produces a certificate, costs per document)?

**Ask your lawyer** if you're signing anything substantial.

### Q18. Checking uploaded documents for viruses

Business customers upload registration papers and licences. Right now we check the file
type and size, but we don't scan for viruses — so an infected file could reach whoever
opens it at your end.

Do you want proper scanning switched on? It's a small monthly cost. Worth it once you're
onboarding businesses you don't already know.

## Legal and policy

These are all running on sensible defaults right now. Read them, change what you disagree
with, and get the whole section past a lawyer before you go public.

| # | The rule | What it's set to now |
|---|---|---|
| Q19 | Someone starts signing up and abandons halfway — how long do we keep the half-finished record before deleting it? | 30 days |
| Q20 | Someone orders without making an account — how long can they come back and pick up as the same person (resume a basket, chase an order, ask about a return)? | 14 days |
| Q21 | Once that window has passed — how long do we hold on to their name, phone and address before deleting them? | 90 days more |
| Q22 | Minimum age to buy | 18 |
| Q23 | Notice period for ending a business contract, either side | 30 days |

**A note on Q21, because it's the one people query.** Deleting those details does *not*
delete the order. Every figure your books need — what was bought, for how much, on what
date, in which area, under which invoice number — is kept permanently. What goes is the
street address and the contact details of somebody who has stopped being a customer.

The reason is that a stored delivery address earns you nothing once the customer is gone,
and costs you a great deal if it ever leaks: for a meal business it's a list of where people
live alongside what they eat, and a medical-style plan says something private about the
person eating it. The rule in privacy law is that you may hold someone's details while you
have a reason to — delivery, returns, disputes, refunds are all reasons — but "it might come
in handy" is not one.

**One thing to check with your accountant:** if Lebanese VAT rules require the customer's
*name* to stay on retained invoices for a set number of years, tell us — that beats the
privacy default and we'll keep the invoice copy intact.

The same principle applies when a customer asks to close their account: their past orders
stay on your books in full, minus the street address.

### Q24. Terms, privacy policy and consent wording

The current terms, privacy policy and consent text are working drafts we wrote. They're
reasonable, but they are not legal advice and they haven't been reviewed by anyone
qualified.

Two things:

- **Who's your lawyer?** These need a proper review before you're taking real customers'
  money and data.
- **The Arabic has to be written by a person, not machine-translated.** Consent that a
  customer can't properly read isn't consent. Do you have someone, or should we source a
  translator?
