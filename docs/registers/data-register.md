## Kitchen catalogue — phase K1.1 (ingredients & allergens)

Every row names exactly one isolation strategy from the vocabulary below. K1.1 adds **no** PostgreSQL policy: the strategies named here describe the intended protection, and in this slice it is enforced at the application layer (global scope, service-layer platform-row rule) and proven by cross-organisation feature tests. The RLS set is unchanged, and `RlsTest` still covers six tables.

| Table | Cluster | Classification | PII | Isolation strategy | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| allergens | Kitchen reference | Public | No | `platform-public-ref` | The 14 canonical EU-14 classes. **`code` is the primary key** — the justified exception to UUIDv7 surrogates (D-023): an allergen class is a regulatory identity that mappings, customer declarations and frozen labels all point at. Never renamed, never deleted; withdrawal is deactivation. Read anonymously through a public projection; written only by a platform operator. **`severe_by_default`** (integration wave, closing an M1 blocker) pre-marks seven classes — crustaceans, egg, fish, peanut, tree nut, sesame, mollusc — as serious on a first declaration form. It is a presentation default and never a diagnosis: what a person's reaction actually is stays on `customer_allergen_declarations.severity`, and nothing overrides it. The other seven are deliberately unmarked — coeliac disease and a milk allergy can both be extremely serious, what they are not is *reliably* so, and a default that cried wolf would train people to clear it without reading |
| ingredient_categories | Kitchen catalogue | Internal | No | `org-rls` (app-scope in K1.1) | Two-level taxonomy in one self-referencing table. `organisation_id` NULL = platform library, visible in every tenant (the `roles` pattern); tenant rows visible only in their own. `UNIQUE NULLS NOT DISTINCT (organisation_id, code)` |
| ingredients | Kitchen catalogue | Internal | No | `org-rls` (app-scope in K1.1) | Platform library (`organisation_id` NULL) plus tenant rows. Carries `lock_version` (optimistic concurrency), `verification_status` (the burghul/pita contradiction is recorded as `requires_review`, never resolved silently) and `source_system`/`source_ref` for import convergence. **No `ingredient_kind`, no `produced_by_recipe_id`** — an intermediate is derived from `recipe_version_outputs` in K1.2 |
| ingredient_aliases | Kitchen catalogue | Internal | No | `join-rls-parent` | Other names an ingredient answers to. `alias_normalised` is model-written (lower/trim/collapse) and is what designation lookup matches; deliberately no stemming or transliteration (risk R4). Reached only through `ingredients`, cascade-deleted with it |
| ingredient_allergens | Kitchen catalogue | Internal (mapping) / Public (the declaration it produces) | No | `join-rls-parent` | Two layers in one table: `organisation_id` NULL = platform baseline, a tenant identifier = that kitchen's overlay. The overlay is **upgrade-only** — a kitchen may add or strengthen, never drop or weaken a baseline row. `market_scope` exists because EU-14 and US Big-9 differ (coconut is US-only). `allergen_code` is `restrictOnDelete` |

`audit_logs.subject_id` widened from `uuid` to `varchar(64)` in the same migration group: allergen classes are the first auditable subject whose identity is a code rather than a surrogate key. The column was never a foreign key — it points at whatever `subject_type` names — so no integrity guarantee is lost.

## Kitchen catalogue — phase K1.2 (recipes & versions)

K1.2 is the first slice to add **PostgreSQL policies** since the foundation: `recipe_versions` and `recipe_version_lines` join the protected set, taking the `RlsTest` pin from six tables to **eight**. They earn a policy where the K1.1 tables did not because a formulation leak is the worst failure this schema can have — the lines *are* a competitor's shopping list — and an application scope is not a defence against a query somebody forgets to scope. The four child tables stay `join-rls-parent`: reachable only through a version, cascade-deleted with it, and covered by the policy above them; four more policies would buy no isolation and would be predicates evaluated on every row of every query.

| Table | Cluster | Classification | PII | Isolation strategy | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| recipes | Kitchen catalogue | Confidential | No | `org-rls` (app-scope; the policy is on the versions) | The stable identity a kitchen names and searches for. Always owned by exactly one organisation — there is no platform recipe library. Carries `lock_version` and `source_system`/`source_ref` for import convergence. **No `current_version_id`**: the partial unique index on the versions is the mechanism, so there is no pointer to fall out of step. `recipe_category` is a plain varchar with **no** CHECK — the source vocabulary describes how one kitchen files its sheets, not a regulated identity |
| recipe_versions | Kitchen catalogue | Confidential | No | **`org-rls` (PostgreSQL policy)** | The unit that is edited, quarantined, published and frozen. `status` CHECK is `draft \| review_required \| published \| retired` — the quarantine state is *in* the CHECK (master plan v2 §4.7), not a flag beside it. `PARTIAL UNIQUE (recipe_id) WHERE status='published'` enforces at most one live version. `derivation_state` + `derived_at` + `derived_input_hash` describe whether the frozen allergen label still matches the mappings it was computed from. `b2b_price_amount` / `b2c_price_amount` / `price_currency_code` are the version's two **list prices** per unit of yield — `decimal(18,6)` in major units (§4.4), one currency for both, `>= 0` so a giveaway is representable and a negative is not. They replaced the recipe editor's single unposted "selling price"; a price on the *recipe* would let a reprice restate what a published version was sold for, so they sit on the version and `newDraft` copies them forward. **The only two columns in this table left out of the model's `Classified(Confidential)` set**, and deliberately: everything else here reads back towards what a formulation costs, whereas a B2C list price is printed on a menu. They are not costs — the cost surface is still K1.3 behind `recipe.view_costs_organisation` |
| recipe_version_lines | Kitchen catalogue | Confidential | No | **`org-rls` (PostgreSQL policy)** | The formulation itself. **No unique key on (version, ingredient)** — duplicate lines are legitimate (different process stages); identity is `(recipe_version_id, line_number)`. `ingredient_id` is `restrictOnDelete` (OD-8), and `IngredientCatalogueService::archive()` carries the same rule one level up. `quantity` is nullable, legally so only while unpublished. Costs are `*_amount decimal(18,6)` in **major** currency units (§4.4), never `*_minor`; **no K1.2 presenter reads them** — the cost surface is K1.3, behind `recipe.view_costs_organisation` |
| recipe_version_outputs | Kitchen catalogue | Confidential | No | `join-rls-parent` | What a version produces (§4.2) — the table that replaced `ingredients.produced_by_recipe_id`, migration 001206 and the fabricated-stub-recipe risk. `UNIQUE (recipe_version_id, ingredient_id)` plus `PARTIAL UNIQUE (recipe_version_id) WHERE is_primary`. An "intermediate" ingredient is simply one that appears here |
| recipe_version_steps | Kitchen catalogue | Confidential | No | `join-rls-parent` | The method, one instruction per row so a step is addressable and `minutes` is a number a scheduler can add up. `instruction_ar` is nullable — blocking a draft on a translation nobody has written would only produce machine translation (§4.18) |
| recipe_version_allergens | Kitchen catalogue | **Public** (the label) / Internal (`source_note`) | No | `join-rls-parent` | The **frozen label**: the one part of a version meant to reach a diner. `derivation` separates `derived` (computed from the effective mappings, with `source_ingredient_id` for provenance) from `declared` (a human statement no mapping implies). A derivation never weakens a declaration. `UNIQUE (recipe_version_id, allergen_code)`; `source_ingredient_id` is `nullOnDelete` because provenance is nice to have and a label row must never be the reason an ingredient cannot be removed |

**Data loading**: none of these tables has a committed seeder. Formulations, yields and costs are confidential kitchen workbook data and arrive exclusively through the private importer (mechanism (c) below); synthetic fixtures for tests are built by factories, never by a data file in this repository.

## Kitchen catalogue — phase K1.3 (recipe costing)

One table, and it is the first in the programme to name **two** isolation strategies at once. `org-rls` keeps one kitchen's margins away from another; `append-only-ledger` — `REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE FROM healthy360_app, healthy360_test`, the treatment `audit_logs` already receives — keeps a kitchen from rewriting *its own* cost history. The policy alone would only have achieved the first, and the failure that actually matters here is the second: a cost history that can be edited after the fact is not a history. The `RlsTest` pin goes from eight tables to **nine**, and a second pin asserts that exactly two tables have their write-back privileges revoked, because a grant is invisible to `pg_policies` and would otherwise be un-regressed.

| Table | Cluster | Classification | PII | Isolation strategy | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| recipe_cost_snapshots | Kitchen catalogue | Confidential | No | **`org-rls` (PostgreSQL policy) + `append-only-ledger`** | What one version cost, at one moment, on one basis. `basis` CHECK is `as_recorded \| recalculated`: `recalculated` is arithmetic this system performed over the lines, `as_recorded` is what a source technical sheet stated **stored verbatim including its errors** — correcting a sheet on import destroys the evidence that it needs correcting, and trusting it puts a wrong number behind a right-looking label. `as_recorded` is written by the private importer only; the API rejects that basis. Amounts are `*_amount decimal(18,6)` in **major** currency units (§4.4) with `currency_code` FK `restrict` beside them; no cross-currency arithmetic exists anywhere. Per-yield-unit and per-piece figures are NULL when the version does not state that yield — a fabricated denominator is exactly what this programme exists to avoid — and CHECKs enforce that a per-unit amount carries its `yield_unit_id` and that a with-waste figure carries its base. `waste_coefficient_percent` is **copied** from the version at calculation time, not joined at read time, so a later revision of the allowance cannot retroactively rewrite an old snapshot. `source_label` + `basis_mismatch` are appendix D findings #1/#2: at least five sheets label a figure "cost per kg" beside a piece count, so **the basis is derived from the version's yield fields and the label is only ever compared against it**, never consulted to decide what a number means. No `updated_at` (model `UPDATED_AT = null`); `INDEX (recipe_version_id, calculated_at)` |

**Cost visibility is a permission, not a projection detail.** `recipe.view_costs_organisation` is separate from `recipe.view_organisation` (appendix C): a line cook reading the method to make the dish must not thereby read the margin on it. Exactly one presenter serialises money — the technical sheet — and a key sweep across the whole non-cost recipe surface fails the build on any response key matching `/cost|margin|amount/`. The same permission gates cost **writes** on `recipe_version_lines`, including the case people miss: replacing a costed line set *without* costs erases them, and positional line identity means they cannot be safely re-attached, so that is refused too. Every successful technical-sheet read is a `catalogue.technical_sheet_viewed` `recordAccess` event with purpose `organisation_administration` and classification `confidential`, carrying identifiers and counts only — an audit row is readable with `audit.view_organisation`, so one amount there would route around the whole split.

**Price visibility is a second, separate permission** — `price_list.view_organisation` / `price_list.manage_organisation` (K1.5), the only kitchen family with a pair of its own. Every other surface reuses `catalogue.*` because ingredients, recipes and listings are one catalogue; prices are not, because price visibility is commercial rather than culinary. A chef writes formulations and a kitchen hand reads them, and neither needs to know what the dish sells for — on an `agreement` list the number is one customer's negotiated position. Folding it into `catalogue.view_organisation` would also have **undone the cost split above**, since a margin is reconstructable from a cost and a price. The kitchen manager and the commercial manager hold both codes; the chef and kitchen staff hold neither, and the seeded grants are test-pinned. Activation reuses `catalogue.publish_organisation`: deciding a tariff goes live is the same authority as deciding what is on sale, and a third publish code would be bookkeeping rather than authority.

**Data loading**: no seeder, for the same reason as K1.2. Costs are confidential kitchen workbook data; the `recalculated` basis is written by the publication path and the explicit snapshot endpoint, and `as_recorded` arrives with the private importer alone.

**Data loading**: `allergens` and the platform `ingredients`/`ingredient_categories`/`ingredient_aliases`/`ingredient_allergens` rows are mechanism (a) — committed, production-safe platform reference seeders (`KitchenReferenceSeeder`). They contain names, categories and regulated allergen classes only: no formulation, no cost, no supplier, no yield. Those arrive exclusively through the private importer (mechanism (c)), never as a file in this repository. The seeders are insert-if-absent, so a platform operator's curation of a seeded row survives the next deployment (risk R8).

## Kitchen catalogue — phase K1.4 (products, meals & channels)

Nine tables, and **no new PostgreSQL policy**. That is the deliberate part. Everything K1.2 and K1.3 protected with a policy is confidential by nature — a formulation is a competitor's shopping list, a cost history is a margin — but a catalogue item is the opposite: its name, its description, its ingredient list and its allergen set are *meant* to reach a diner, and publishing one is the act of saying so. What is genuinely sensitive about a catalogue is what it costs, and that lives in K1.5's `price_lists`, where the decision is K1.5's to make. The `RlsTest` pin therefore stays at **nine** tables; these nine are `org-rls` in vocabulary and app-scope in mechanism, with cross-organisation isolation proven by feature test rather than by a predicate evaluated on every row of every query.

**No table in this family has a price, a cost, a margin or a currency column, and none of the presenters has such a field.** The admin contract's confidential `marginPercent` is a *computed presentation figure* — gross margin against a recipe cost per serving at the current confirmed price — with both of its inputs outside this slice, so it is recorded here as a reconciliation note rather than modelled server-side.

| Table | Cluster | Classification | PII | Isolation strategy | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| diet_classifications | Reference data | **Public** | No | `platform-public-ref` | How a person describes the pattern of what they eat — the vocabulary an item is tagged with and `GET /reference/diet-classifications` serves anonymously. Twelve codes, matching the frontend `DIET_CLASSIFICATIONS` union 1:1 and forming the superset the source workbook's nine fold into. **Surrogate key, not a code PK**: the allergen table's `code` PK is the justified exception it says it is (a regulatory identity that must stay self-describing in a food-safety context); a diet tag is a marketing filter, so the house rule (D-023) applies with `code` as a unique key beside it. A classification is a **preference filter, never a medical restriction** — nothing in this programme reads a diet tag to answer an allergen question. Arabic is authored and pending native review; the seeder is insert-if-absent so that review survives deployment (R8) |
| product_categories | Kitchen catalogue | Internal | No | `org-rls` (app-scope in K1.4) | How a kitchen files what it *sells*. Deliberately not `ingredient_categories`: that taxonomy is purchasing-shaped (62 nodes, two levels, "where does this raw material live"), this one is merchandising-shaped (10 nodes, flat, "which shelf of the shop"). They agree today and will not agree for long. `organisation_id` NULL is the platform library, `UNIQUE NULLS NOT DISTINCT (organisation_id, code)` — the `ingredient_categories` pattern |
| sales_channels | Kitchen catalogue | Internal | No | `org-rls` (app-scope in K1.4) | A route to market. **A row, not an enum**: the frontend's closed `SalesChannel` union is right for a prototype and wrong for the database, because two kitchens legitimately run two different wholesale desks with different availability and different price lists, and a union value cannot be owned by an organisation. `channel_kind` CHECK (`b2c_web \| b2b \| pos \| marketplace \| corporate \| insurance`) is the closed vocabulary a projection branches on; `code` is the kitchen's own name for one instance of it. `organisation_id` NOT NULL — there is no platform channel library, because a channel is a commercial arrangement. `order_source` is unconstrained free text on purpose: it is somebody else's vocabulary. `status` is `active \| inactive`, never the sellable family — nothing a customer sees is a channel |
| catalogues | Kitchen catalogue | Internal | No | `org-rls` (app-scope in K1.4) | The container an organisation's items belong to, thin by design: it exists so a kitchen can hold a summer menu beside the live one without duplicating every item's identity. `branch_id` is `nullOnDelete` — closing a branch must not delete the range it was selling. `status` is `draft \| active \| archived`, **operational**: a catalogue is a folder, and giving the folder a publication state too would create two places to ask "is this live" |
| catalogue_items | Kitchen catalogue | **Public** (name, description) / Internal (`review_reason`, `data_quality_flags`, provenance) | No | `org-rls` (app-scope in K1.4) | Everything a kitchen sells, one table with `item_type` CHECK (`product \| meal \| subscription_plan`). One table because the alternative gives every consumer of a sellable thing three price paths, three availability tables and three publication gates; what genuinely differs is nullable columns and child tables. **`status` is the SELLABLE family** — `draft \| review_required \| published \| retired` (§4.7) — not the operational pair the rest of this module uses, and `review_required` is a stored quarantine inside the CHECK. Sellable items **retire, never archive**. `slug` is immutable after creation (§4): a rename changes names only, because a slug is what a link and a partner integration hold. `recipe_id` and `ingredient_id` are both `nullOnDelete` — a catalogue row must never be the reason an ingredient cannot be removed. `is_market_priced` / `is_assorted` are facts about the article that pricing *reads*, not price columns. `data_quality_flags` is a jsonb array of unresolved import findings. `UNIQUE (organisation_id, slug)` + partial-unique-if-sourced |
| catalogue_item_variants | Kitchen catalogue | **Public** | No | `join-rls-parent` | The thing a price actually points at: `variant_type` CHECK (`pack \| plan_configuration`) — **one pricing path** (appendix D), because packs and plan configurations are the same shape from pricing's point of view. `status` is `draft \| active \| archived`, **operational and deliberately not the sellable family**: a pack is not separately published, since publishing the 500 g jar while the 1 kg jar sits in draft is not a state a kitchen wants. `PARTIAL UNIQUE (catalogue_item_id) WHERE is_default` — "which pack do we show first" must have exactly one answer, enforced by PostgreSQL rather than by a service promising to clear the incumbent first |
| catalogue_item_pack_variants | Kitchen catalogue | **Public** | No | `join-rls-parent` | The physical detail of a pack. **PK is `catalogue_item_variant_id`**: a pack *is* the variant seen from the warehouse, so one-to-one is the fact and one-to-one is the constraint. Split from the variants table rather than nullable columns on it, because a plan configuration has no `pack_quantity` and never will. `pack_format` CHECK (`bottle \| bag \| can \| gallon \| bunch \| loose`) is nullable *and* carries `loose`, because "the kitchen said loose" and "nobody has said" are different facts. `net_weight_grams` is nullable and **never derived** from `pack_quantity`: that conversion needs a density this system does not hold |
| catalogue_item_ingredients | Kitchen catalogue | **Public** | No | `join-rls-parent` | What an item is made of *as a customer is told it*. **No quantities, and there will not be**: quantities are the formulation, the formulation is confidential, and a table carrying both a public list and a quantity would be one careless presenter away from publishing a competitor's proportions. Also the **allergen basis of last resort** — the fallback the derivation uses when no published recipe version exists, which is why the publish gate accepts either. `ingredient_id` is `restrictOnDelete` (OD-8) and the usage registry carries the same rule one level up |
| catalogue_item_diet_classifications | Kitchen catalogue | **Public** | No | `join-rls-parent` | The tag pivot. `diet_classification_id` is `restrictOnDelete` — the vocabulary is platform reference data and one in use is not one to remove; deactivation is the supported withdrawal. Surrogate key + denormalised `organisation_id` on a pure join table, for consistency with every other child here and so a future policy needs no join |
| channel_catalogue_items | Kitchen catalogue | Internal | No | `join-rls-parent` | Which items a channel offers, and when. A row is a statement; its **absence says nothing** — availability and publication are different questions, and an item can be published and complete while no channel offers it yet. `UNIQUE NULLS NOT DISTINCT (sales_channel_id, catalogue_item_id, catalogue_item_variant_id)` so "the item on this channel" and "this pack on this channel" are two statements neither of which can be made twice. Dates, not timestamps: a kitchen decides "from Monday". **No price column** — what an offering costs is a `price_list_items` row in K1.5, effective-dated separately, because a price changes far more often than an offering does |
| price_lists | Pricing | Internal | No | `org-rls` (app-scope in K1.5) | A named set of prices. **The currency lives here, not on the row** (master plan v2 §4.4): every amount inside a list is denominated in `currency_code`, so a total is a sum of comparable numbers by construction and there is no cross-currency arithmetic to arch-test away. A kitchen selling in two currencies keeps two lists, which is what it actually has. The currency becomes immutable the moment the list holds its first item row — including *closed* rows, because history negotiated in the old currency cannot be re-denominated at all — and the refusal is `409 resource.conflict` with `reason: currency_locked`. `customer_scope` CHECK (`public \| agreement`) is the confidentiality boundary the module draws, and a column rather than an inference from the channel kind because one channel legitimately carries both. `status` is `draft \| active \| archived`, **operational and deliberately not the sellable family**: a price list is not published content, it is the instrument that prices content. `valid_from`/`valid_to` are **inclusive at both ends** — a human writing "valid to 30 June" means through the 30th — which is deliberately not the convention `price_list_items.effective_to` uses. Header only: the numbers that need a policy are one table over |
| price_list_items | Pricing | **Confidential** | No | **`org-rls` (PostgreSQL policy)** | One price, for one pricing point, over one interval of time — and the table the policy set exists for. A negotiated price says what a kitchen will accept, from whom, and how much room it has left. **Effective-dated supersession, never an update in place**: a change closes the standing row (`effective_to` set, `superseded_by_id` → its replacement) and inserts a new one, so what a customer was charged last March stays reconstructable. `effective_to` is **exclusive** — forced, not chosen: a replacement made today closes the incumbent at today and opens its successor at today, so an inclusive end would leave two rows both claiming the day of the change. A same-day change therefore leaves a zero-length interval, which is honest. `PARTIAL UNIQUE (price_list_id, catalogue_item_id, catalogue_item_variant_id, min_quantity) NULLS NOT DISTINCT WHERE effective_to IS NULL` makes "the current price of this point" single-valued while leaving history unconstrained; `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` matters as much as the partiality, since a variant-less and a tier-less row both carry NULLs. **CHECK `(price_status = 'confirmed') = (unit_amount_minor IS NOT NULL)`** — written as an equality of two booleans so it refuses a confirmed row with no number *and* a placeholder carrying one; this is what makes the honest badge (OD-2, R9) something no import or backfill can subvert. `unit_amount_minor bigint` in **minor units** with the currency on the parent list. `min_quantity` is the tier threshold, NULL for the base price, normalised to four decimals so `12` and `12.0` are one point. `catalogue_item_id`/`catalogue_item_variant_id` are `restrictOnDelete` — deleting an article that has ever been priced is refused at the database, because the price row is the record of what was charged. **Not an append-only ledger**, deliberately: the diff has to close standing rows, which is an UPDATE, so the `audit_logs` revoke would break supersession on the first price change; the narrower immutability (history is closed, never rewritten or deleted) is enforced by the service and the partial index |
| channel_price_lists | Pricing | Internal | No | `join-rls-parent` | Which tariffs a channel prices from, and in what order it consults them. A channel may hold several on purpose — a standing trade tariff plus a negotiated sheet for one account — and `priority` (**lower consulted first**) encodes it. Resolution stops at the first list that prices the point rather than merging them, so an agreement can override two lines without restating the other four hundred and a gap in it falls through rather than becoming a hole. Priority is assigned from the submitted array order: the order a merchandiser lists tariffs in *is* the order they mean them consulted in. `UNIQUE (sales_channel_id, price_list_id)` — stating the relationship twice at two priorities is a contradiction, not a richer configuration. **No `is_active` flag**: a switched-off assignment is a row that prices nothing while looking as though it does, and the thing that genuinely goes quiet — the channel — already has a status. Carries no amount |

**Allergens are derived on read, never stored on an item.** Two bases in order of authority: a linked recipe's **published** version's frozen label (authoritative — derived from the whole formulation at publication and only ever strengthened by a human declaration), otherwise the union of the item's own listed ingredients' effective mappings with the strongest containment winning. An item with neither reports `basis: none`, which means "nobody has said" and never "no allergens". There is no roll-up column and no per-item allergen table in K1.4: the joins are cheap, and a stored copy goes stale the moment a mapping changes. Stored labels exist exactly where freezing is the point — on a published recipe version — and K1.8 revisits this when the recompute job and the readiness evaluator arrive together.

**Withdrawal interlocks.** The K1.2 `IngredientUsageRegistry` port is now answered by two modules: recipes bind an implementation, catalogues **decorate** it, and the composed answer carries `recipe_ids`, `recipe_version_ids` and `catalogue_item_ids`. An ingredient a non-retired item lists cannot be archived; a published recipe version a **published** item sells cannot be retired (a draft item does not block — drafting next month's menu must not obstruct withdrawing this month's). Both refuse with `catalogue.in_use` naming what to withdraw first.

Both are insert-if-absent (R8). The demo tenant gets three sales channels — a web shop, a wholesale desk and a counter (the third so that *switching a product onto* a channel is demonstrable at all: the product catalogue seeder puts every product on the two that sell it) — structure only; **no item, no price and no formulation is seeded anywhere**, and none ever will be.

## Kitchen catalogue — phase K1.6 (commercial plan definitions)

Six tables, and **no new PostgreSQL policy**. The `RlsTest` pin stays at ten. That follows the K1.4 decision rather than repeating it: a plan's configurations, its calorie bands and the lengths it is sold for are all things a customer is meant to see, so they are `org-rls` in vocabulary and app-scope in mechanism. What is confidential about a plan is what it costs and what a longer commitment takes off that, and the first already lives in `price_list_items` behind a real policy. `plan_variant_durations.discount_percent` is the one genuinely commercial column added here; it is classified `Internal` and reaches no anonymous surface, and if a public plan projection ever needs a discounted price it will be computed behind the pricing permission rather than by exposing this column.

**Variant existence encodes the availability matrix.** There is no grid table and no `is_available` flag on a cell: a combination × tier × band a kitchen sells is a `catalogue_item_variants` row of type `plan_configuration` with a `plan_variant_profiles` row on it, and one it does not sell is the absence of one. That is what keeps the thing a customer picks and the thing a price points at a single object — a plan is priced through exactly the same path as a pack, and a matrix modelled beside the variants would have needed a second pricing path to be worth anything.

**§4.3 lands here.** `plan_durations.duration_kind` replaces the zero-day sentinel outright, and the CHECK is written as the full disjunction with an explicit `IS NOT NULL` — without it `NULL > 0` evaluates to NULL, a CHECK passes on anything that is not FALSE, and a `fixed_days` row with no length would slip through the constraint that exists to stop exactly that. 5, 20, 40 and 60 days remain ordinary rows.

## Kitchen catalogue — phase K1.7 (delivery configuration & kitchen operating data)

Five tables — one platform gazetteer, three delivery tables and the branch operating table the v1 catalogue missed — and **no new PostgreSQL policy**. The `RlsTest` pin stays at ten. That follows the K1.4 and K1.6 decisions rather than repeating them: where a kitchen delivers, what it charges to get there and when it is open are all printed on its own checkout page. What is confidential in this programme is what things *cost the kitchen*, and that already lives behind a policy in `recipe_cost_snapshots` and `price_list_items`.

**The geography is two levels, and the split is the design.** `delivery_areas` is a platform gazetteer with **no `organisation_id` at all** — two kitchens serving Achrafieh serve the *same* Achrafieh, and that shared identity is what lets an address captured in J1 be matched against any kitchen's zones without re-entry. `delivery_zones` is each kitchen's own commercial statement over those places. Collapsing the two would give every kitchen a private copy of Lebanon and make the join J1 and C1 need impossible.

**One area is served by one zone per branch** (master-plan rule), enforced by `UNIQUE (organisation_id, branch_id, delivery_area_id) NULLS NOT DISTINCT` on `delivery_zone_areas` with `branch_id` denormalised from the parent zone at write time — a unique index cannot reach through a join. What the key *permits* is as deliberate as what it forbids: an organisation-wide claim (`branch_id NULL`) and a branch-scoped claim on one area coexist, because NULL and a branch identifier are different values. That is the **override mechanism**, and it makes the resolution order load-bearing: `ZoneResolver::zoneFor($area, $branch)` resolves **branch before organisation-wide**, consults only the organisation-wide map when no branch is in context, and does **not** fall back from a suspended branch zone to the organisation map — "we have paused deliveries here" is a decision the system must not overrule. J1's address validation calls that resolver rather than re-deriving the rule, because two implementations of a precedence are two precedences.

## Order desk — phase C2 (payment receipts and plan menus)

Two tables, and **no new PostgreSQL policy**. The `RlsTest` pin stays at eleven. That is not the K1.4 argument repeated — a receipt is nobody's published content — it is the `orders` argument inherited. A receipt is reachable only through an order; `OrderQuery::forSeller()` is the only kitchen-facing way to an order and it never forgets its organisation filter; and a policy on the child of an unprotected parent protects nothing that resolving the parent has not already decided. Unlike an order there is no customer-facing read path here at all, which is why the strategy is plain `org-rls` rather than the "mine *or* my organisation's" predicate `customer_accounts` needed. The table also does **not** take the `append-only-ledger` revoke, and that is the more interesting refusal: it *is* append-only — no update route, no delete route, no negative amount — but by **convention** rather than by grant. The ledgers that carry the revoke are evidence *about* the system which the system itself must not be able to rewrite (an audit trail, a cost history, the movement record behind every COGS figure). A desk receipt is an operational record with one writer, no reader outside the kitchen that wrote it, and a correction path that is a manager and a phone call rather than an UPDATE. The pin exists so that another ledger has to be argued for; the argument does not carry here, so convention is the honest strength of the guarantee and convention is what is claimed.

**This is also the phase that widened `orders.payment_method`.** The single-value CHECK laid down in C1 stated its own terms of release: widening it would be "a deliberate migration in the phase that introduces a second way to pay, alongside the tables that make paying that way possible". So the CHECK moved to three values — `cash_on_delivery | cash_at_counter | wish` — in the same migration that created the table below, under the same constraint name, and `OrderArchitectureTest` moved both of its enum assertions with it in the same commit. What did **not** change is the harder rule: still no card, token, provider, gateway or status column anywhere on `orders`.

Archiving would additionally keep `(day, slot, sequence)` occupied, turning "Tuesday's lunch is the fish now" into revive-and-repoint instead of a plain statement. **`slot` is a plain vocabulary word, not a foreign key** — `breakfast \| lunch \| dinner \| snack`, the four `subscription_meal_choices` stores, agreed by a shared CHECK rather than by a reference to `meal_combination_options`: that table's own migration makes the argument (a kitchen re-pointing a combination must not mean last Tuesday's lunch was really a dinner), and it is stronger here because a menu is what the production run is planned from. `sequence` is that table's column verbatim, for the kitchen that sells "lunch, twice". **`UNIQUE (catalogue_item_id, cycle_day, slot, sequence)`** with no `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` clause, because unlike a matrix cell every coordinate of a menu is stated and none of the four is nullable; two rows at one address is how a subscriber receives two lunches. **The cycle is anchored to the plan, not to each subscriber**: day 3 is the same calendar Wednesday for everybody on the plan, which makes a day one production run and a forecast a sum rather than a per-subscriber simulation, and lets a customer be told what is for dinner on Thursday. The cost — a subscriber joining on day 5 starts at day 5 — is what a fixed menu means. Cycle arithmetic lives in `PlanMenuEntry::cycleDayFor()`, double-modulo so dates *before* the anchor resolve inside the cycle instead of to a day zero. `created_by` is `nullOnDelete` |

**Data loading**: no seeder. A menu is a kitchen's own decision about what it cooks, and a seeded one would be a claim about a real kitchen's production. `DemoTenantSeeder` leaves every plan menu-less, which is also the state that keeps the demo's subscription orders deducting nothing. Test fixtures are built by `PlanMenuEntryFactory`.

## Standing conventions

- UUIDv7 primary keys, application-generated via the central identifier service; ISO-code PKs for stable reference tables (Plan §8).
- Tenant-owned tables carry `organisation_id` (and `branch_id` where applicable), `created_by`, timestamps; `lock_version` where optimistic concurrency matters.
- Retention periods and deletion/anonymisation rules are **not yet decided** (OQ-002); crypto-shredding and jurisdiction-specific deletion remain documented designs (Plan §12).
- Deferred tables (tax, integrations, webhooks, billing, commissions, settlements, kitchen, nutrition, clinical) may appear in future ERDs but are not migrated in this phase.

## Data loading — three mechanisms, and only three (D-046)

Every row that reaches a database arrives by one of these, and the mechanism is stated wherever data is described:

1. **Platform reference seeders** — committed to the repository, non-confidential, production-safe: allergen classes, measurement units, the delivery-area gazetteer, currencies, countries, languages and other controlled vocabularies.
2. **Synthetic fixtures** — committed, demo and test only, and labelled synthetic on screen. They contain **no real formulations, costs or supplier terms**; a synthetic nutrition or cost figure is never promoted to production data.
3. **Private tenant importer** — an artisan command reading confidential source data from an out-of-repo path supplied at run time, with a manifest and sha256 checksums, a dry-run mode, created/skipped/failed counts, unresolved-alias, data-quality, allergen-review and quarantine reports, insert-if-absent semantics so operator edits are never overwritten, idempotent re-runs, environment allowlisting and an audited execution record.

**Confidential source data is never committed to this repository in any form** — not as a seeder, not as a JSON fixture, not as a test resource, and not behind an environment guard. A local-only guard on a committed file still commits the file.

### Mechanism (c) as built — `kitchen:import-workbook` (K1.8)

The command lives in the **Kitchens** module (`app-modules/kitchens/src/Console/ImportKitchenWorkbookCommand.php`) and is registered only when running in the console, so no HTTP path reaches it. Catalogues could not host it — Pricing depends on Catalogues, so a Catalogues → Pricing edge would close a cycle — and Kitchens is the only K1 module nothing that may hold code depends on. Its registry `depends_on` was widened in the same commit rather than left describing branch hours.

| Feature | As built |
|---|---|
| Signature | `kitchen:import-workbook --source=<dir> [--dry-run] [--validate-only] [--org=<slug>]` |
| Environment allowlist | `config('kitchens.import.environments')`, default `local,testing`, overridable by `KITCHEN_WORKBOOK_IMPORT_ENVIRONMENTS`. Refuses elsewhere with a printed explanation and a non-zero exit — never a silent no-op |
| Manifest | sha256 + byte length of all five source files, recorded in the run report and compared against the most recent previous report. A changed file is a **named warning**, not a refusal: insert-if-absent is safe against a changed source, and refusing would block the operator who just fixed a typo the last run reported |
| Provenance | every created row carries `source_system = healthy360_workbook` plus a `source_ref` naming file, sheet and row; the existing partial unique indexes on `(organisation_id, source_system, source_ref)` are what make a re-run converge |
| Unit of idempotency | the organisation's **slug**; a **source reference** for ingredients, recipes, versions, catalogue items and price rows; a **code** for channels, tariffs, catalogues, vocabularies, zones and windows. Children (lines, outputs, packs, member ingredients, channel availability, variant profiles) carry no reference of their own and are written **only** when their parent is created — which is exactly what stops a re-run overwriting an operator's edit |
| Dry run | executes the entire write path inside a transaction that is always rolled back, so "would create" means "did create, and then did not". A second implementation of the insert-if-absent rules would have been a second thing to keep in step |
| Validate only | parses and reports; never opens a connection to the write path at all |
| Reports | console **and** a JSON document under `storage/app/import-reports/` (gitignored by `storage/app/.gitignore`'s catch-all). Six registers: counts (created / would-create / skipped_existing / failed per entity), unresolved designations with every occurrence, incomplete sheets, data-quality findings, the allergen-review escalation, quarantine and known gaps |
| Audit | `catalogue.workbook_import_started` and `catalogue.workbook_import_finished`, purpose of use `organisation_administration`. The finish event carries counts and **counts of problems** — never the problems, because an audit row is readable with `audit.view_organisation` and would otherwise become a second, less guarded copy of the report |
| Transaction | one per run. A half-imported kitchen is worse than none: the counts would look plausible and the gaps would be invisible |

**The curated designation dictionary** ships at `app-modules/ingredients/database/data/kitchen-workbook-aliases.json` — with the ingredients module, which owns designation resolution, not with the module that happens to run the command. It is human-authored and the importer never adds to it. There is no fuzzy match, no edit distance and no plural stripping anywhere in the import path, because no string metric separates "Cripsy Spice → Crispy Spice" (a typo) from "Sweet Paprika → Paprika" (a different product). Four sections: `aliases` (spelling, case, word order and the appendix D pairs), `tenant_ingredients` (the rows the platform library does not carry, created unverified and unmapped), `recipe_links` / `recipe_links_declined` (which product is made by which technical sheet, and which candidate links a human refused, with reasons), and `never_merge` (sets that look mergeable and are not).

**The one place this system does not fail loudly** is designation resolution — recorded as a deviation in the decisions register. An unresolved designation costs its own recipe line, is reported by name with every sheet it appears on, and flags its sheet incomplete. It does not fabricate an ingredient and it does not abandon the file. §4.11 asks the importer for created / skipped / failed counts, and those only mean something if a run survives a failure.

## Isolation strategy vocabulary (D-047)

Every table added from phase K1 onwards names **exactly one** strategy in its register row. The vocabulary is closed; "application scoped" is not a member of it:

| Strategy | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `platform-public-ref` | Platform-owned reference data, readable by everyone, written only by a platform operator |
| `org-rls` | Tenant data protected by a PostgreSQL row-level-security policy on `organisation_id` |
| `user-owner-rls` / `customer-owner-rls` | Rows owned by one person or one customer account, protected by an owner-matching policy |
| `join-rls-parent` | Child rows reached only through an RLS-protected parent — FK cascade plus a denormalised organisation id so the policy can be evaluated directly |
| `platform-only` | Reachable only through an explicit platform-operator permission; no tenant read path exists |
| `capability-token` | Rows reachable only by presenting an opaque, capability-limited token issued by a service (guest sessions and the rows they reach) |
| `append-only-ledger` | Insert-only, with `UPDATE` and `DELETE` revoked from the application role |

The twenty-three foundation rows above predate the vocabulary; each is annotated when its isolation next changes. RLS tests run under the non-owner role (`rls` group, `SET ROLE`) and extend in every phase that adds a policy.

**Tables carrying a PostgreSQL policy today (eleven, pinned by `RlsTest`)**: `organisation_branches`, `organisation_memberships`, `roles`, `feature_entitlements`, `consent_grants`, `audit_logs` (foundation), `recipe_versions`, `recipe_version_lines` (K1.2), `recipe_cost_snapshots` (K1.3), `price_list_items` (K1.5) and `customer_accounts` (J1, with a third arm for ownerless guest rows — D-070). Every new tenant-scoped table decides at migration time whether it joins the set — the pin exists so the decision is deliberate rather than inherited. **K1.4 added nine tables and none of them joined**, on the stated ground that a catalogue item is published content: that was the decision rather than an omission. **K1.5 added three and exactly one joined**, on the mirror ground — a price is the opposite of published content — so the boundary now runs through the middle of the catalogue rather than around it, which is where the confidentiality actually changes. **K1.6 added six and none of them joined**: a plan's configurations, bands and durations are things a customer is meant to see, and the one commercial column among them (`plan_variant_durations.discount_percent`) is `Internal` and reaches no anonymous surface. **K1.7 added five and none of them joined either** — a delivery fee and a set of opening hours are printed on the kitchen's own checkout page, and `delivery_areas` is `platform-public-ref` with no tenant column to protect at all. **The final backend wave gave read paths to four more — `subscriptions`, `account_closure_requests`, `b2b_offboardings` and `record_exports` — and none of them joined either (D-071)**, each declined for its own reason rather than by one blanket answer: a policy on the first two would have made the generation sweep and the scheduled-closure safety net read zero rows *silently*, and `org-rls` on the last two is simply the wrong word — their only reader is a platform operator whose selected organisation is never the row's, so their strategy is `platform-only`. **The Order Desk (C2) added `order_payment_receipts` and it did not join either**, on the narrowest ground yet: it is a child of `orders`, which has no policy itself, so a predicate here would be evaluated on every row of every query to protect something the parent lookup has already decided. **Nor did `plan_menu_entries`**, for the K1.6 answer rather than that one — a menu is content a customer is meant to see, and it is reachable only through an item that carries no policy for the same reason.

**Tables carrying the `append-only-ledger` revoke today (two, pinned by `RlsTest`)**: `audit_logs` and `recipe_cost_snapshots`. A revoked grant leaves no trace in `pg_policies`, so it needs a pin of its own; without one, a later migration granting `UPDATE` back would restore write access silently. `price_list_items` is named in the same pin as a **near-miss that must stay writable**: it is the most confidential table in the schema and still not a ledger, because the supersession diff closes standing rows with an UPDATE and a revoke would break it on the first price change.
