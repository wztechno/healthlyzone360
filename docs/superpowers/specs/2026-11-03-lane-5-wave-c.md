# Lane 5: P1 Wave C, daily money discipline

Date: 3 November 2026. Built overnight on `P0-Stage1`. Tests are written and not run, per the brief. Nothing was exercised in the app.

## Built

1. **Lane 2's gaps.** Refunds come off revenue and margin in the monthly report, with a new `refunds_amount`. Bell alerts for a short hand-in and for the requester when the owner decides a refund. Driver runs from before settlement are off the list.
2. **Daily close.** `daily_closes` and `/kitchen/order-desk/close`: float, count and variance per shift, in the kitchen's own days. A short close needs a reason and a manager's sign-off, and alerts the managers.
3. **Price control and payables.** Each priced line stores the last price and the difference. A rise past the kitchen's limit waits for approval and alerts the owner. Due dates come from a new `suppliers.payment_due_days`. `/kitchen/payables` lists what is owed, and new permission `inventory.approve_payables_organisation` lets the owner, administrator and finance approve and mark paid.
4. **Dish margin.** `dish_margins`, `/kitchen/dish-margins`, a target per kitchen, and a nightly `RefreshDishMargins` job that alerts the owner through `StaffAlerts`.

## Decisions

**Refunds**
1. A refund lands in the month the money went back, not the month of the sale, so a month somebody has read does not move.
2. Only completed entries of kind `refund` count. A `receipt_correction` means a payment was written down by mistake, and the food was still sold. A refund on a cancelled order is left out, because its revenue was never counted.
3. Revenue is the food alone and a refund can include the delivery fee, so each order's refunds count up to its subtotal, tracked across months.
4. Revenue can go negative in a month with refunds and no sales. The line-of-business split stays gross, because a refund names no line.
5. **Analytics needed no change.** It carries order counts and units, no money, as Lane 1 found.

**Alerts**
6. Another lane added `subjectType()` and `page()` on `StaffAlertType` at the same time, and I built on those. Alert rows keep the `order_number` key for the reference a non-order alert names: a date, a supplier or a dish. Rows exist under it, and it is still not personal data.
7. A short hand-in has no reference, because the driver's name is personal data.
8. `requester` is a third audience, read through a new `NoticeRecipients::member()`. An owner deciding their own entry hears nothing.

**Settlement cut-off**
9. A flag written once by the migration (`delivery_jobs.predates_settlement`), not a rolling window, which would quietly drop a real unsettled run as it aged.
10. Runs delivered more than two days before the migration are marked. The last shift or two stay on the list.
11. Driver runs only. A courier's receipt is written by settling, so an old courier run is money the order still shows as owing.

**Daily close**
12. A shift is everything since the last close, one till per kitchen per currency. Nothing opens a shift. The first close starts at the local midnight. Receipts and hand-ins carry no branch, so a till per branch cannot be read yet.
13. The figures read the rows the cash report and settlement read: non-Whish receipts, `handed_in_minor`, completed refunds. They are stored at the close, so the record is what was signed.
14. Road cash counts at the hand-in, never at the door. A receipt confirmed by the run's driver is excluded, and so is a receipt written after a courier's batch settled.
15. A hand-in counts net of a courier's fee and any short, since that is what came through the door.
16. A cash refund comes out of the till, whichever cash method it names. A receipt correction counts only when the receipt it corrects is in the same shift. An older one was never in this till, and that close already came up short for it.
17. A shift's day is the local day it opened on, so an evening past midnight stays on its day.
18. A count above expected is recorded with no sign-off. The ruling only covers short.
19. A shift with nothing in it is refused, which also stops a second press writing a second shift.
20. The float is typed at the close, prefilled from the last float. There is no separate open step.
21. The cash report itself stays on UTC days. Moving it is a separate change, and it would change a report staff already read.

**Price control**
22. Approval holds the payment, never the goods or the cost. Receiving has never been lost to a price, and the blend is already a fact.
23. The limit starts empty, which turns approvals off. No ruling names a number, and a default would hold up invoices on a figure nobody chose.
24. The comparison is the last priced delivery of that shelf before this one, from any supplier, same currency and same unit. Otherwise the variance stays empty. Units are not converted.
25. Only rises wait. A receipt already approved goes back to pending if a line priced later is over the limit.
26. Approval is approve-only. A disputed price stays pending, which holds the payment. Correcting a posted price remains out of scope, as §3.6 says.
27. One new code rather than the owner's refund code: `finance_manager` holds this and not that.
28. The alert goes to owners, as refunds do. Finance gets to the page but not the alert.

**Payables**
29. One payment per receipt, no part payments. Nothing sends money.
30. Every receipt on file at the migration becomes `not_tracked`, or the first list would claim the kitchen owes for everything it ever bought.
31. A direct purchase with no supplier is `not_tracked`: it was paid on the day. A price rise on one still shows for approval.
32. Amount is the invoice total when keyed, otherwise the lines. It is empty while a line has no price, and such a row cannot be paid.
33. `payment_terms` stays free text. The new number sits beside it.
34. The due date runs from the invoice date, or the received date. Without a number on the supplier it stays empty until someone sets it.
35. The list is capped at 200 unpaid rows. Marked in the code.

**Dish margin**
36. Stored per dish and channel, because costing a whole menu on a page load is one query walk per recipe. The figures are as of last night, and the page says so.
37. A dish is a published catalogue item that is not a resale product or a plan.
38. Price and cost are the technical sheet's: the published version's list price and `WeeklyRecipeCostingService`, per unit of yield, packaging included.
39. Withheld, with a reason, for no published recipe, no list price, an incomplete cost, or two currencies. A recipe with no lines is incomplete, not free.
40. The target starts empty, for the reason in 23.
41. **The alert fires only when the cost moved the dish below target**, as the brief says. A dish found low because the target rose, the price fell, or on its first run is flagged on the page and alerts nobody. Otherwise setting a target would mail the owner once per dish. This means a dish that was already low when the kitchen first runs never alerts until it recovers and falls.
42. Daily at 02:00 UTC. A cost move surfaces the next morning.

## Skipped

- **Rejecting a price, and correcting a posted price.** The second needs its own reasoned, audited adjustment, and the owner has not said what a rejected delivery should do.
- **Part payments, supplier statements, ageing buckets.** Add them when a kitchen pays one invoice in stages.
- **A till per branch.** Needs a branch on receipts or a till on the desk.
- **Unit conversion in the variance.** Add it when a kitchen buys one shelf in two units.
- **Variance in the purchases ledger and on receiving.** It shows on the payables page only.
- **Local days on the cash report.** See 21.
- **Refund bell for an owner who requested their own.** They completed it.
- **No card or Whish Checkout code**, per the brief.
